# Funding announcement

A funding announcement shows progress and potential. The point is the strategic impact of the money, so the plan should frame everything around what it unlocks, not the amount.

## Ask first (one batch, skip what is already known)

- Round size, round type (pre-seed, seed, Series A), and whether the round has closed. Always ask this directly. If it has not closed yet, keep planning, but remind the founder to make sure it is fully closed before anything is announced, and put that reminder at the top of the checklist.
- Lead investor and other participants, including angels, and whether each has agreed to be named
- What the money is for, in specifics (hires, markets, product), and one real proof point (revenue growth, customers signed, pilots, retention)
- Whether investors plan their own posts or blogs, and when
- Customer type, since it affects whether a customer newsletter helps or hurts

## Core checklist items

**Prep and approvals**
- Confirm the round has closed before anything goes public. Owner: founder.
- Messaging doc. Purpose: align everyone and feed social posts. Needs: one sentence on the news plus approved supporting facts. Owner: founder. Lives in a Google or Word doc.
- Align with investors on messaging, timing, and any co-announcements. Investors need a week to a week and a half if they want to write their own blog post. Owner: founder and investors.
- Images. Needs: a photo of the founders, and an image stating the vision and what the funding enables. Keep branding consistent and sized for LinkedIn. Give investors screenshots or founder images for their blogs. Owner: founder.
- Short, personal copy ready for employees, and short copy for investors that reflects their confidence in the company's vision. Owner: founder, with each person editing in their own words.

**Day of**
- Founder LinkedIn post from a personal account, then the brand page post. Employees and investors share their own, tagging the company and investors.
- Note to investors that it is live, with links to the blog post (if any), sample social copy, images, and the brand and founder posts.

## Optional items (offer one at a time)

- **Blog post.** Worth it when there is something to say that should stick around: what the money will do, how it affects current and future customers, any third-party support. Not required just because a round was announced. Needs: funding amount, investors, vision. Publish on the company site. Avoid late Friday or late at night, and supporting posts can follow the next day.
- **Press release.** Only if the founder will pitch media. Needs: quotes from the CEO, at least one investor, and a customer if possible, tailored for media. Even unsent, it helps get approvals and align facts. Apply the media reality check and local-first approach from the main file.
- **Customer newsletter.** Can signal that the company is well funded and staying. It can also feel jarring, for example sharing a $2M raise with massive enterprise customers. Needs: summary of the news, how the funds improve the customer experience (features, support, speed), and links to relevant updates.
- **Stakeholder newsletter** for mentors, advisors, and partners. Existing investors already know, but the founder should own the story other stakeholders might see. Needs: the round, investors and amounts, how it fits the strategy, and thanks to the people who helped.
- **VC newsletter pitches.** Read `vc-newsletter-pitch.md`.
- **Press release or pitch to local and trade press,** only if a press release is in the plan.
