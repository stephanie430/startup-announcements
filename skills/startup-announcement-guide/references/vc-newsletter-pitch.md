# VC newsletter pitches

Deal roundup newsletters run short items about funding rounds. Editors want facts they can paste in: the company, what it does, round size, who led, who participated, and where it is based. A short note that makes that easy to use is what gets included.

## The pitch format

Write one version per newsletter. Do not send one blast to several publications.

> We would love to have the following round included in the next [Newsletter name] deal roundup. Please let me know if you need any additional details.
>
> [Company name](company link), the [plain-language description], today announced the close of a [$X] [round] led by [lead investor], with participation from [other investors]. The company is HQ'd in [city, state].
>
> Thank you for considering it for your roundup! Please let me know if you have any questions or if I can provide additional details.

Example (the company and investors are made up, use the founder's real facts):

> [Brindle Health](https://example.com), the AI-native scheduling platform for independent physical therapy clinics, today announced the close of a $3 million seed round led by Marlowe Hill Capital, with participation from North Fork Ventures, Quillfeather Partners, and angel investor Dana Whitcombe. The company is HQ'd in Omaha, Nebraska.

### Details that matter

- **Description.** Push back on vague ones. "AI-native revenue operations platform" tells an editor what the company does. "Innovative platform for modern teams" does not. Ask for a rewrite.
- **Investors.** List the lead, then the other funds, then angels by name after the funds. Confirm each investor agreed to be named.
- **Round.** Ask whether the round has closed. Say "close of" only if it has. If it has not, remind the founder to make sure it is closed before the announcement and hold the pitch until it is.
- **Optional extra line.** If the round is larger or there is a strong proof point, offer one more line on traction or use of funds. Otherwise leave it out. Roundups are fact lists, and extras can get trimmed.
- **Subject line.** Offer one, such as "Funding announcement: [Company] raises [$X] [round]". Keep it plain.
- **Timing.** Send the day the news goes live, not before.

## Which newsletters

National trio: Term Sheet, Axios Pro Rata, and WSJ Pro. Their deal roundups often include rounds well under $20M, so offer them for any closed round. The $20M line in the media advice is about dedicated coverage, meaning a reporter writing a story, which is a different thing. Be honest that inclusion is an editor's call and never guaranteed, but the pitch is cheap to send and the odds in a roundup are much better than for a standalone story.

For everything else, find outlets through search instead of relying on a fixed list, because newsletters launch and shut down.

1. Ask for the company's city and sector, and which outlets the founder already reads.
2. If web search is available, search for newsletters and publications in that city and sector that run funding roundups or deal news. Check local first (business journals, metro papers, local startup and tech newsletters), then industry trades.
3. For each candidate, look on the outlet's own site for a tips or submissions address or form. Report where you found it.
4. Present the results as suggestions to verify. Never present an email address as confirmed unless it came from the outlet's own page. Drop anything that does not appear to take submissions.
5. If web search is not available, say so up front and ask the founder which outlets they read, then draft pitches for those and tell them to check each outlet's current submission instructions.

Do not hardcode submission addresses into drafts. They change.
